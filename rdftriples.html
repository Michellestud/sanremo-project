GENERATING NEW TRIPLES

Once we had analysed the outputs obtained from querying the AI assistants, we transformed the retrieved data into RDF triples. Considering that the Sanremo Music Festival was not represented in ArCo, we had to introduce entirely new knowledge into the ontology network rather than extending existing data. To represent the cultural-heritage information and encode it as RDF triples, we adopted Turtle (Terse RDF Triple Language), a human-readable RDF serialisation format.

We created 159 triples so as to build a sufficiently comprehensive database on Italy's most relevant music festival.

First of all, we wrote the prefixes using the official namespaces associated with the ontologies and vocabularies employed in our dataset, then we defined the first properties of Sanremo, such as its name in both Italian and English, as well as its recurrent and annual nature.

Subsequently, we specified the location where the festival is held, namely the Ariston Theatre, and we represented the event as a proper competition with an associated award, referred to as the "Leone d'oro" or "Trofeo del Festival di Sanremo".

We then defined the entity "76th edition of Sanremo" as part of the festival, specifying its start and end dates and its hosts. For the latter, we relied on properties and links from Wikidata, since equivalent properties were not available in ArCo. We later specified that Laura Pausini, host of the last edition of the festival, is a singer by linking her to the corresponding "singer" entity in Wikidata.

We identified the name by which the festival is most widely known internationally.

We then defined a restriction on the "participant" property, which is imported from Wikidata, and proceeded to enumerate the festival's participants.

OWL adds semantic meaning and logical relationships to the data previously represented through RDF triples. By using owl:oneOf, we created a closed class, as the festival had already concluded and therefore no additional participants could be incorporated.

We adopted the "performance" property from Wikidata and defined its domain as "Musical Work" and its range as "Singer", in accordance with the domain and range already existing for this property. In this way, we asserted that all subjects of this property are songs (musical works), while its objects are singers.

We also described singers as subclasses of cpv:Person.

The entities representing the singers and songs participating in the 2026 Sanremo Music Festival were identified on Wikidata and linked through the wdt:P175 predicate we just mentioned. We then specified the names of the songs through rdfs:label and the names of the artists through IO:name. The same procedure was repeated for all 30 competing songs and their respective singers.

After listing all the competing singers along with their corresponding songs, we used the wdt: predicate from Wikidata (which we named "hasWinner") to define the winner of the 2026 Sanremo Music Festival. We did not repeat the artist's name again, as it had already been mentioned when listing all the competing singers and their songs.
